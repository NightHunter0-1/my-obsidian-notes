**Verbs / Phrases / Idioms:**

- **to strike a chord with:** To cause someone to feel sympathy, enthusiasm, or agreement. It resonates with them on a personal or emotional level.
    
    - _Example: "Her song about loneliness really **struck a chord with** young people."_
        
- **to trot out a trite phrase:** To use a phrase or saying that is overused and has lost its original meaning or impact. It suggests a lack of original thought.
    
    - _Example: "The manager just **trotted out the trite phrase** 'think outside the box' instead of giving real advice."_
        
- **to get hung up on:** To be overly concerned, worried, or obsessed with something, to the point that it prevents you from making progress.
    
    - _Example: "Don't **get hung up on** one small mistake; just keep going."_
        
- **to go along with smb:** To agree with someone or their idea, or to do what they suggest, often without strong personal conviction.
    
    - _Example: "I didn't really like the plan, but I decided to **go along with** my friends."_
        
- **to have fair share of smth:** To have a typical or expected amount of something, often something difficult or unpleasant.
    
    - _Example: "Every entrepreneur **has their fair share of** failures before they succeed."_
        
- **to give it a good go:** To try something with a lot of effort and determination, even if you don't ultimately succeed.
    
    - _Example: "I may not win the race, but I promise I'll **give it a good go**."_
        
- **to be cut out for smth:** To have the natural ability or personality suitable for a particular task or role.
    
    - _Example: "She's a great vet because she's very patient with animals; she's really **cut out for** the job."_
        
- **to make strides:** To make significant and steady progress towards a goal.
    
    - _Example: "The company is **making strides** in developing new green technology."_
        
- **to fly in the face of:** To directly oppose or contradict something (like evidence, tradition, or common sense).
    
    - _Example: "His theory **flies in the face of** all established scientific knowledge."_
        
- **to change tack:** To try a different method or approach because the current one is not working.
    
    - _Example: "The marketing campaign wasn't working, so they decided to **change tack**."_
        
- **to throw in the towel:** To give up; to admit defeat. (From boxing, where a trainer throws a towel into the ring to stop the fight.)
    
    - _Example: "After years of trying to make the business profitable, he finally **threw in the towel**."_
        
- **to knock confidence:** To damage or reduce someone's self-belief.
    
    - _Example: "Failing his driving test really **knocked his confidence**."_
        

**Adjectives / Nouns / Expressions:**

- **spot on:** Exactly right; completely accurate.
    
    - _Example: "Your analysis of the problem was **spot on**."_
        
- **way too simplistic:** Excessively simple; not considering the complexity of a situation.
    
    - _Example: "His solution of 'just work harder' is **way too simplistic** for such a complex issue."_
        
- **a sweeping statement:** A general statement that is too broad and lacks nuance, often unfairly applied to a whole group or situation.
    
    - _Example: "Saying 'all politicians are corrupt' is a **sweeping statement**."_
        
- **a complete fallacy:** A belief or argument based on a false idea; something that is completely untrue.
    
    - _Example: "The idea that you only use 10% of your brain is a **complete fallacy**."_
        
- **an element of truth:** A small part of a statement or argument that is true, even if the whole thing is not.
    
    - _Example: "While I think his conclusion is wrong, there's **an element of truth** in what he says about social media."_
        
- **vacuous comments:** Remarks that lack intelligence or substance; empty and meaningless.
    
    - _Example: "The celebrity was famous for making **vacuous comments** during interviews."_
        
- **patently not true:** Obviously and clearly false.
    
    - _Example: "The claim that the Earth is flat is **patently not true**."_
        
- **cringeworthy:** Causing a feeling of embarrassment or awkwardness that makes you "cringe."
    
    - _Example: "His attempt to dance with the teenagers was **cringeworthy**."_
        
- **resilient:** Able to recover quickly from difficult conditions; tough.
    
    - _Example: "Children are often very **resilient** and can adapt to new situations easily."_
        
- **to pursue a career:** To try to get a job in a particular profession and make progress in it over time.
    
    - _Example: "She moved to London to **pursue a career** in finance."_
        
- **It bugs me when I hear…:** It annoys or irritates me when I hear...
    
    - _Example: "**It bugs me when I hear** people chewing loudly."_
        
- **galling:** Annoying or humiliating; causing intense irritation.
    
    - _Example: "It was **galling** to see my rival get the promotion I deserved."_
        
- **to have dire consequences:** To have very serious, terrible, or disastrous results.
    
    - _Example: "A small error in the software could **have dire consequences** for the entire system."_
